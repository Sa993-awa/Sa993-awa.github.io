##### During this unit, I developed my understanding of Object-Oriented Programming (OOP) and its role in designing structured and reusable software. Initially, I found concepts such as classes, objects, and OOP principles challenging, but through practising examples and experimenting with Python code, I gradually became more confident and found the topic more enjoyable.

##### I applied the four main principles of OOP: inheritance, polymorphism, encapsulation, and abstraction. I used inheritance to create a vehicle class hierarchy, applied polymorphism through geometric shape classes, practised encapsulation by protecting data in a bank account class, and explored abstraction through animal classes with different behaviours. These activities helped me understand how OOP concepts are applied in real programming tasks.

##### The practical exercises involving constructors and destructors improved my ability to write Python code independently and understand how objects are created and managed. I learned that OOP helps developers create software that is organised, reusable, secure, and easier to maintain. This understanding is supported by Sridhar, Indumathi and Hariharan (2023), who explain the importance of Python classes and object-oriented principles in building structured code.

##### Overall, this unit improved my programming confidence and changed my view of OOP from a theoretical concept into a practical approach for developing better software.
