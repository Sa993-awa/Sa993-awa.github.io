### 💬 Unit Reflection

This week introduced me to concurrency and parallelism in object-oriented design, expanding my understanding of how modern software handles multiple tasks at once. I learned that good design is not only about organising code but also about keeping applications reliable when many operations happen at the same time.

The key concepts were threads, synchronisation, race conditions, and deadlocks. In my practical work I built a thread-safe banking system where each account had its own lock, so deposits, withdrawals, and transfers stayed accurate even when several users ran concurrently. I prevented race conditions by protecting the shared balance with locks and avoided deadlocks by acquiring locks in a consistent order. My concurrent tests confirmed this, always producing the correct final balance regardless of thread order.

At first I found managing shared resources and understanding race conditions challenging, but experimenting with synchronisation improved my confidence. I used encapsulation which helped control access to shared data, and separating the BankAccount and TransactionSimulator classes kept the design clean, following the Single Responsibility Principle.

Overall, this unit improved my understanding of how scalable, responsive systems are built. Concurrency is challenging but essential, and these skills will be valuable for designing secure, high-performance applications in future projects.
